Service 01

Concrete Power Floating

Power floating is the machine-driven process of trowelling a freshly poured concrete slab to produce a dense, smooth, and level surface free of water channels, air voids, and surface laitance. The result is a floor that resists dusting, abrasion, and chemical contamination — critical for logistics, retail, and industrial environments.

Vuka Construction operates its own ride-on and walk-behind power trowels, allowing us to handle slabs of any size without subcontracting machinery. We work to mix designs ranging from 25MPa to 40MPa depending on the imposed loads and use case.

✓ SANS 10249 slab flatness compliance
✓ Laser-level base establishment on all projects
✓ Industrial hardener & curing compound included
✓ Suitable for narrow-aisle forklift operations
How We Do It

The Power Float Process

1

Sub-base & Formwork

Prepared sub-base compacted to specification. Formwork set to laser-established datums. Reinforcement (mesh or rebar) placed and supported at correct cover depth.

2

Concrete Pour & Strike-off

Ready-mix concrete placed and struck off level with screed boards or a laser-guided wet screed machine. Concrete allowed to reach initial set — typically 1–3 hours depending on temperature.

3

Pan Float Pass

Walk-behind or ride-on power trowel with pan float fitted makes the first pass. Closes surface, embeds coarse aggregate, and begins the densification process.

4

Finishing Blade Passes

Pan replaced with finishing blades. Multiple passes at increasing blade pitch produce the final dense, burnished surface. Edge work completed by hand float.

5

Hardener / Densifier

Penetrating silicate hardener applied immediately after floating. Chemically reacts with concrete to increase surface hardness, reduce dusting, and improve abrasion resistance.

6

Curing Compound

Spray-applied curing compound prevents premature moisture loss during the 28-day curing period, ensuring the concrete achieves its full design strength.

Service 02

Polished Concrete

Polished concrete is a multi-step diamond grinding and sealing process that transforms a dull concrete slab into a dense, reflective surface that rivals natural stone. It is low-maintenance, extremely durable, and eliminates the need for tiles, vinyl, or carpet — and the grout lines, adhesive failures, and replacement costs that come with them.

Vuka Construction uses a 7-stage diamond system — from 40-grit metal bond for initial grinding through to 1500-grit resin bond for final polish — producing results from a subtle matte cream finish to a high-gloss showroom floor.

✓ Matte, satin, or high-gloss finish levels available
✓ Cream, salt-and-pepper, or full aggregate exposure
✓ Penetrating densifier applied — no topical coating to peel
✓ Suitable for new slabs and existing concrete floors
How We Do It

The Polishing Process

1

Assessment & Preparation

Slab assessed for cracks, previous coatings, embedded contaminants, and surface hardness. Cracks filled with matching epoxy filler. Previous coatings ground off with coarse diamonds.

2

Coarse Grinding (40 → 80 grit)

Metal-bond diamond segments remove surface laitance, level the slab, and begin exposing the aggregate layer. Level of aggregate exposure set at this stage.

3

Medium Grinding (150 grit)

Resin-bond diamonds refine the surface, removing coarse scratch pattern from the previous step. Grouting compound applied to fill pinholes and surface voids.

4

Densifier Application

Penetrating lithium silicate densifier applied and allowed to cure 4–24 hours. Chemically reacts with free calcium in the concrete to fill pores and increase surface hardness.

5

Fine Grinding (400 → 800 grit)

Progressive fine-grit resin diamonds build reflectivity. This stage determines the final sheen level — 400 for matte, 800 for satin.

6

Sealer & Guard Application

Penetrating or topical sealer applied for stain resistance. For high-gloss, a burnishable guard is applied and buffed to final sheen.

Where It's Used

Applications

Residential Living Areas

Open-plan living, dining, and bedroom floors — tile-free and grout-free.

Service 03

Exposed Aggregate Concrete

Exposed aggregate concrete reveals the natural texture and colour of the decorative aggregate embedded in the concrete surface. By applying a chemical surface retarder during the pour and pressure-washing before full set, we expose the top layer of aggregate to create a textured, slip-resistant, and visually distinctive surface.

The result is a surface that looks premium, handles outdoor UV and weather exposure without fading, and provides natural slip resistance — making it the preferred choice for driveways, pool surrounds, and outdoor entertaining areas across South Africa.

✓ Choice of aggregate type, size, and colour
✓ Natural slip resistance — ideal for pool areas
✓ UV and weather-stable — no fading or peeling
✓ Channel drains and step edging available
How We Do It

The Exposed Aggregate Process

1

Excavation & Sub-base

Area excavated to correct depth. G5 sub-base material placed and compacted. Formwork set to establish correct level and falls for drainage.

2

Concrete Pour

25MPa concrete poured and struck off level. Reinforcement mesh laid at correct cover depth before or during pour.

3

Aggregate Seeding

Selected decorative aggregate (river pebble, quartz, granite chip) broadcast evenly over the wet surface and floated in to produce an even distribution.

4

Surface Retarder Application

Chemical retarder sprayed onto the surface and covered with polythene sheeting. Retarder delays set of the surface paste while the body of the slab hardens normally.

5

Pressure Wash

After 12–24 hours, polythene removed and surface pressure-washed to expose the aggregate and remove retarded paste. Depth of exposure controlled by timing.

6

Sealing

UV-stable penetrating sealer applied to the cured surface, enhancing the colour of the aggregate and providing protection against staining and weathering.

Applications

Service 04

Slab Waterproofing

Water ingress through flat slabs is one of the most common and damaging building failures in South Africa. A correctly applied waterproofing system prevents moisture penetration, protects structural reinforcement from corrosion, and eliminates the interior damage and mould problems that follow a leaking slab.

Vuka Construction applies bituminous torch-on membranes, liquid-applied membranes, and crystalline waterproofing systems — selecting the right product for the specific exposure condition, detail complexity, and budget of each project.

✓ Torch-on SBS membrane systems
✓ Liquid-applied acrylic and polyurethane membranes
✓ Full detailing at upstands, penetrations, and drains
✓ Flood-test on completion before handover
How We Do It

The Waterproofing Process

1

Diagnosis & Surface Prep

Slab inspected for cracks, failed joints, and blocked drainage. Existing failed membrane removed. Cracks routed and filled with flexible polyurethane filler. Surface cleaned and dried.

2

Primer Application

Bitumen primer applied to the prepared slab surface and allowed to dry fully. Primer creates the bond between the concrete substrate and the waterproofing membrane.

3

Base Layer Membrane

SBS-modified bitumen base sheet torched onto the primed slab, overlapping seams by 150mm. Membrane extended up all upstands and around all penetrations.

4

Cap Sheet Layer

Granulated cap sheet torched over the base layer. Two-layer system provides redundancy — if one layer is compromised, the second layer maintains waterproofing integrity.

5

Detailing & Flashing

All penetrations (pipes, cable ducts) and upstand corners receive additional membrane flashing. These are the highest-risk points for water ingress and receive the most attention.

6

Flood Test & Handover

Drainage outlets plugged and slab flooded to 50mm depth for 24 hours. Any active leaks identified and repaired. Client sign-off only after flood test passes.

Where It's Used

Applications

Flat Roof Slabs

The most common application — preventing water from penetrating into the building below.

Balconies & Terraces

Exposed balcony slabs with drainage falls and upstand flashings.

Basement Floors & Walls

Below-grade waterproofing to prevent groundwater ingress and rising damp.

Wet Room Floors

Bathroom, shower, and laundry floor waterproofing before tile installation.

Planter Boxes

Waterproofing concrete planters on podium decks to prevent root damage and water ingress.

Swimming Pool Surrounds

Podium deck waterproofing around swimming pools and water features.

Painting & Coatings

Vuka Construction delivers professional interior and exterior painting services using premium SABS-approved coating systems. Every project begins with thorough surface preparation — because no paint system performs better than the surface it's applied to — and ends with a clean, fully covered finish that protects and transforms the structure.

We also apply industrial epoxy floor coatings, which provide chemical resistance, ease of cleaning, and a professional finish for workshop floors, garages, and pharmaceutical or food production environments.

✓ Full surface prep included — crack fill, plastering, sanding
✓ Alkali-resistant primer on all masonry surfaces
✓ Two-coat finish coat system as standard
✓ Epoxy floor coatings available for industrial applications
How We Do It

The Painting Process

1

Surface Preparation

High-pressure washing to remove dirt, algae, and chalk. Loose plaster hacked off and patched. Cracks filled with acrylic flexible sealant or patching compound. Sanded smooth where required.

2

Masking & Protection

All adjacent surfaces, windows, door frames, and fixtures masked with tape and dust sheets. Flooring and planted areas covered and protected before any spray or brush work begins.

3

Primer Coat

Alkali-resistant primer applied to all masonry surfaces. PVA sealer on porous plaster. Etch primer on galvanised metal. Full coverage, no holidays — primer is the most important coat in the system.

4

First Finish Coat

Premium acrylic or water-based alkyd applied at correct spread rate. Applied by brush, roller, or spray depending on surface type and job specification.

5

Second Finish Coat

Second coat applied after the recommended recoat time. Two coats are non-negotiable on all external surfaces — a single coat will not provide adequate film thickness or weather protection.

6

Touch-up & Inspection

Final walk-around with the client. All missed areas, holidays, and drips corrected before sign-off. Site left clean — all masking, drop sheets, and waste removed.

Service 06

Paving

Paving is only as good as what's underneath it. Vuka Construction starts every paving project with a correctly prepared sub-base — the most commonly skipped step by cheap paving contractors — and finishes with proper edge restraints, correct jointing, and compaction. This is why our paving stays level, does not sink, and does not shift at the edges.

We supply and install concrete block paving, clay brick paving, and natural stone paving in a range of patterns including herringbone, stretcher bond, and basket weave. We also manage channel drain installation, kerbing, and step construction as part of the same scope.

✓ G5 sub-base compaction to specification — no shortcuts
✓ Concrete block, clay brick, and natural stone options
✓ Herringbone, stretcher, and basketweave patterns
✓ Channel drains, kerbing, and step construction included
How We Do It

The Paving Process

1

Excavation

Area excavated to correct formation depth — typically 250mm below finished paving level. Existing material removed and disposed of. Formation level checked for soft spots and stabilised.

2

Sub-base Compaction

G5 sub-base placed in 100mm compacted layers and compacted with plate compactor or roller. Minimum 150mm G5 for pedestrian areas; 200mm for vehicle-rated paving. This is the most critical step.

3

Bed Layer

30mm screeded sand bed or cement-stabilised sand bed applied over the compacted sub-base. Bed established to correct level and cross-fall for drainage (minimum 1:50 fall away from buildings).

4

Paver Laying

Pavers laid to agreed pattern, cut at edges with angle grinder or guillotine. Soldier course or soldier-and-sailor edging established at perimeter. Level and line checked throughout.

5

Edge Restraints & Kerbing

Concrete haunching or pre-cast kerbing installed at all free edges to prevent lateral movement of pavers. Haunching cured before the next step.

6

Jointing & Compaction

Kiln-dried jointing sand brushed into all joints. Plate compactor run over the entire paved area to bed pavers and lock joints. Top-up sand brushed in and compacted a second time.
